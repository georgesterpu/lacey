# Lacey: a friendly pair of eyes

**Lacey** is pronounced “lay-see”. It connects to shoelaces and reads like the friendly person in the club who always spots a bargain. The name, wordmark and tone are working product choices, not a claim that a domain or trademark is available.

The character is a tiny pair of smiling soles: warm yellow, lavender and little rosy cheeks. It is an SVG built into the page, not another image service, SDK or runtime dependency. The illustration is decorative and hidden from screen readers. Hover movement respects reduced-motion preferences.

The voice is a little cheeky, warm and brief. “We do the looking. You take the credit 😊” is the idea. Use a smile in the welcome, a successful save and the email sign-off, rather than making every control a joke. Keep errors, money, availability and unsubscribe actions easy to understand. Nobody should have to decode a joke to stop an email.

Examples:

- Hero: “Happy feet. Cheeky prices.”
- A new watch: “Saved! Your future feet say thanks 😊”
- Empty results: “No matches. Yet 👀”
- Email: “Ooh. These look promising 😊”
- Sign-off: “Happy miles, Lacey 😊”

Retail terminology: **Department** means Men’s, Women’s, Kids’ or Adults / unisex. Size stays separate. Width remains a shoe property; the old internal `fit` field is retained for storage/API compatibility and is not used as a consumer-facing label.

The inspiration is the human wit in [Mailchimp’s voice guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) and the small character moments in [Duolingo’s design work](https://blog.duolingo.com/building-character/). The Lidl receipt example supplied the direction for a simple, warm smiling sign-off. No supermarket design, asset or receipt text is copied.
